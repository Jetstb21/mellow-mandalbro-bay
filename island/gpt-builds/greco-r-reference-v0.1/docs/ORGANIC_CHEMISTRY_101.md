## Six fundamental tools

1. **Valence and electron counting.** Neutral carbon commonly makes four covalent bonds, nitrogen three bonds and one lone pair, oxygen two bonds and two lone pairs, H one bond.
2. **Formal charge:** valence electrons - nonbonding electrons - number of bonds (count line bonds, double counts twice).
3. **Electronegativity/polarity:** oxygen generally draws shared electron density more than carbon; this makes C=O carbon electrophilic.
4. **Lewis acids/bases:** acceptor/donor of an electron pair. Brønsted acids/bases transfer a proton; these definitions are related but distinct.
5. **Curved arrows:** electron pairs move from lone pair or bond toward an electron-poor atom/bond. Never draw an electron-flow arrow as if atoms themselves are moving.
6. **Resonance:** two Lewis contributors represent one delocalized electronic structure. Atoms do NOT physically alternate between contributors; only electron distribution differs.

## Worked example: acetate

CH3-C(=O)-O⁻ ↔ CH3-C(-O⁻)=O

Both oxygen positions share the negative charge in the resonance hybrid. You cannot treat these as two independently observable molecules rapidly interconverting. The carbon skeleton stays fixed. This makes acetate unusually stable compared with a localized alkoxide.

## Practice test — 10 questions (answer before opening key)

1. How many covalent bonds does neutral carbon normally form?
2. Formal charge of nitrogen with four single bonds and no lone pair?
3. Which atom is more electronegative, C or O?
4. In a carbonyl, which atom is more electron-poor: C or O?
5. What is the difference between resonance contributors and conformational isomers?
6. Give the formal charge for oxygen with one single bond and three lone pairs.
7. In CH3OH, is O-H generally polar? Which end is relatively negative?
8. Which is a Lewis base: ammonia NH3 or proton H+? Explain by lone pairs.
9. Why is acetate less basic than ethoxide, broadly speaking?
10. Which organelles and ions participate in heart muscle excitation-contraction coupling? Identify Ca2+ and SR rather than invoking a unique emotion chemical.

## Answer key

1. Four. 2. +1. 3. O. 4. Carbon. 5. Resonance changes only the electron assignment in an unchanged nuclear framework; conformers change spatial atom arrangement by rotation about bonds. 6. -1. 7. Polar, O relatively negative. 8. NH3 donates its lone pair. 9. Acetate is resonance stabilized; ethoxide's charge is more localized (solvent context also matters). 10. Ca2+ release and uptake by sarcoplasmic reticulum and cytosolic calcium signaling; it is not defined by a single hormone.

## What mastery looks like

You can draw a valid Lewis structure; assign every charge; distinguish resonance from dynamics; predict electron-flow direction; justify acid-base behavior; and identify when a physiological phenomenon is mechanical, electrophysiological, biochemical or photonic. Repeated problem solving matters more than memorizing thousands of molecules.
