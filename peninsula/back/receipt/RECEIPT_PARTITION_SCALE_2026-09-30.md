## Answer

No. Height is not a uniform scale factor.

The extra centimeters in a proportionally tall woman go mostly into the legs. The trunk and head do not grow by the same ratio. Two women of the same stature can still have different leg-to-torso splits. About 29% of one adult-woman sample sat more than one standard deviation off the average hip-height ratio. Same height, different partition.

Sitting-height ratio falls as stature rises in the tall tail. Dutch charts already use that. A "perfect" tier is inside the band, not a photograph of a scaled-up mean.

Pelvis is its own partition.

- Overall pelvic size tracks stature (about r = 0.73 in women).
- Shape does not. Taller people average a relatively taller, narrower pelvis.
- Birth-relevant shape (subpubic angle, acetabula spacing) is mostly non-allometric. It does not simply enlarge because she is tall. Puberty hormones, not stature, carry that dimorphism.

So: bigger woman, somewhat bigger pelvis, not a bigger copy of the birth geometry.
